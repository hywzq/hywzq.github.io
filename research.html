Research Portfolio研究专栏

Focusing on monsoon dynamics and vegetation–climate feedbacks, with paleoclimate modelling, Earth system model development and paleoclimate data–model comparison as the three core foundations, this research explores how orbital forcing and vegetation change jointly shape monsoon precipitation and climate variability from the middle Pleistocene to the 21st century. 以季风动力学与植被—气候反馈为核心，以古气候模拟、地球系统模式开发与古气候数据—模式对比为三大基础，探究轨道强迫与植被变化如何共同塑造从中更新世到 21 世纪的季风降水与气候变率。

Methods Across the Portfolio整体方法框架

The portfolio combines numerical climate modelling with paleoclimate data analysis. Coupled climate–vegetation experiments in EC-Earth3/4 are compared against proxy records — loess, aeolian deposits, tree rings and pollen — and against existing PMIP4 and PlioMIP3 simulations. Model output is then analysed statistically to separate forced responses from internal variability. 研究结合数值气候模拟与古气候数据分析。EC-Earth3/4 的气候—植被耦合试验与代用记录（黄土、风成沉积、树轮与孢粉）以及已有的 PMIP4、PlioMIP3 模拟结果进行对比，并通过统计分析区分强迫响应与内部变率。

Vegetation–Climate Feedbacks植被—气候反馈

Monsoon precipitation is not driven by orbital forcing alone — vegetation responds to climate and then feeds back on it. These studies isolate that feedback in coupled climate–vegetation simulations and test how its strength differs between interglacials. 季风降水并非仅由轨道强迫驱动——植被在响应气候之后又会反过来影响气候。本方向在气候—植被耦合模拟中分离这一反馈，并检验其在不同间冰期的强度差异。

Differential vegetation feedback on the global land monsoon system during the Mid-Holocene and Last Interglacial

Advances in Atmospheric Sciences, 2026. Shows that vegetation feedback strengthens the global land monsoon, but with a strength that differs between the Mid-Holocene and the Last Interglacial.发现植被反馈整体增强全球陆地季风，但在全新世中期与末次间冰期的强度存在差异。

Climate Variability and Teleconnections气候变率与遥相关

Internal variability is often as large as the forced signal. These studies examine AMOC behaviour, ENSO persistence and interhemispheric radiative forcing, and what they mean for extremes and for the confidence of future projections. 内部变率的幅度常可与强迫信号相比。本方向研究 AMOC 行为、ENSO 持续性以及半球间辐射强迫，及其对极端事件与未来预估可信度的意义。
